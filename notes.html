Subject Code: BBC102 | Unit - 1: Foundation of Information Systems

1. Foundation of Information Systems (IS)

Introduction: An information system is a systematic way to work with information using computers and digital technology, combining computer programs, physical devices, and networks.

  • Businesses use IS to collect crucial data and execute operations smoothly.
  • Helps enterprises communicate effectively with customers and maintain a competitive edge.
  • Global tech giants like Amazon, Google, Alibaba, and eBay are built entirely on IS frameworks.

2. Components of Information Systems

  • Hardware: Physical tangible equipment like computers, servers, keyboards, and mobile devices.
  • Software: Programs running on hardware (System software like Windows & Application software like MS Excel).
  • Data: Raw facts and figures that become meaningful insights when processed properly.
  • Telecommunications: Networking elements connecting computers and devices via wires or wireless signals.

3. How Information Systems Work (Processing Cycle)

  • Input: Capturing data via typing, voice commands, touchscreens, or IoT sensors.
  • Processing: CPU organizes raw data through sorting, grouping, searching, and computation.
  • Storage: Retaining processed data temporarily or permanently in databases and hard drives.
  • Output: Delivering useful insights via management reports, dashboards, or charts.
  • Feedback: User interactions and response evaluation to measure system performance.

4. Types of Information Systems & Management Pyramid

Information systems support various organizational tiers from operational staff to executives:

  • Management Support (Executive Level): Executive Information Systems (EIS), Decision Support Systems (DSS), Management Reporting Systems (MIS).
  • Support of Knowledge Work: Professional support systems, Collaboration tools, and Knowledge management systems.
  • Operational Support: Supply Chain Management (SCM), Customer Relationship Management (CRM), Transaction Processing Systems (TPS).

5. Solving Business Problems & Case Study

IS solves operational bottlenecks by automating routine tasks, improving customer experience, and streamlining supply chains.

Coffee Shop POS Case Study Example: A small coffee shop experienced low profits. By implementing a Point of Sale (POS) system, they tracked item sales and peak time slots. Action was taken to reduce pastry waste and introduce afternoon combos, resulting in a 30% reduction in food costs and a 25% increase in afternoon sales!

6. Effectiveness vs Efficiency Criteria

  • Effectiveness ("Doing the right things"): Focusing on achieving strategic organizational goals and user satisfaction.
  • Efficiency ("Doing things right"): Optimizing resources, maximizing output with minimal time, cost, and waste.
